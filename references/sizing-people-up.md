# Sizing People Up Operating Reference

This reference adapts trust-as-predictability concepts into a context-specific meeting-analysis lens. It is about whether commitments are reliable in this situation, not whether a person has good or bad character.

## Main Use In The Skill

Use this reference when assessing commitment reliability, follow-through risk, stakeholder alignment, or trust-but-verify actions after a meeting.

## Signals To Look For

- Trust as predictability: whether the participant's future behavior can be reasonably anticipated from evidence.
- Vesting: what the participant has at stake in the outcome.
- Longevity: whether the relationship, project, or dependency is ongoing.
- Reliability: past or current follow-through evidence in the transcript.
- Actions: concrete steps taken or promised, not just stated intent.
- Language: specific commitments, deadlines, qualifiers, vague agreement, or passive phrasing.
- Stability: consistency of position, criteria, and commitments across the conversation.
- Self-interest alignment: whether the requested action aligns with what the participant appears to value, protect, or need.
- Trust-but-verify: practical confirmation steps such as written recap, owner check, deadline check, or proof of completion.

## Output Sections It Supports

- Trust behavior prediction
- Decision accountability
- Risk register
- Relationship risk
- Next meeting playbook
- Commitment and owner review

## Guardrails

- Do not judge a person's character.
- Do not call someone trustworthy or untrustworthy in general.
- Do not infer dishonesty.
- Do not use liking, confidence, warmth, seniority, or charisma as trust evidence by itself.
- Treat trust as task-specific: trust for what, under what conditions, based on what evidence.

## Confidence Rules

- High confidence: the transcript contains clear actions, specific commitments, and consistent follow-through evidence.
- Medium confidence: the participant makes specific commitments but follow-through evidence is incomplete.
- Low confidence: the transcript contains vague language, missing owners, or limited history.
- Reduce confidence when incentives, pressure, or constraints are unclear.

## When Not To Use This Reference

- When the transcript has no commitments, dependencies, or future behavior to assess.
- When the user asks for moral judgment, character judgment, or deception detection.
- When the source is too incomplete to identify roles, stakes, or actions.
- When the appropriate output is simply "verify before relying."
