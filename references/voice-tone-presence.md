# Voice Tone Presence Operating Reference

This reference adapts professional voice and speech-improvement concepts into a meeting-analysis lens. Use it only when the source includes delivery evidence.

## Main Use In The Skill

Use this reference to evaluate communication clarity, pace, voice strength, hesitation, over-explaining, authority, warmth, and self-review opportunities in meeting transcripts with tone, pause, interruption, audio, or delivery markers.

## Signals To Look For

- Clarity: direct wording, clean structure, clear asks, and concise summaries.
- Pace: rushed transitions, slow responses, long pauses, or time-pressure markers.
- Voice strength: audible, steady, and appropriately emphatic delivery when markers are available.
- Hesitation: false starts, repeated qualifiers, pauses before key answers, or self-correction.
- Over-explaining: repeated justification, excessive detail after acceptance, or answering beyond the question.
- Authority: calm framing, decisive summary, clear ownership, and ability to close a topic.
- Warmth: acknowledgment, appreciation, inclusive language, and repair after tension.
- Self-review: places where a speaker could improve clarity, brevity, timing, or closing strength.

## Output Sections It Supports

- Voice, tone, and presence observations
- Communication style map
- Next meeting playbook
- Recommended opening and closing
- Transcript quality caveats for tone confidence

## Guardrails

- Do not infer tone from text alone unless the wording itself directly supports the claim.
- Do not treat hesitation as weakness; it may reflect care, complexity, translation, audio delay, or legal caution.
- Do not equate volume with authority or confidence.
- Do not judge accent, dialect, speech difference, disability, or language fluency.
- Keep recommendations practical and respectful.

## Confidence Rules

- High confidence: delivery markers are explicit and repeated, and transcript quality is strong.
- Medium confidence: some delivery markers exist but audio or context is incomplete.
- Low confidence: the transcript is mostly text and tone must be inferred from wording.
- No confidence: there are no tone, pause, interruption, or delivery markers.

## When Not To Use This Reference

- Plain text transcripts with no delivery evidence.
- Requests to judge personality, intelligence, credibility, or honesty from voice.
- Situations where audio quality, translation, or accessibility factors dominate the evidence.
- When the task only needs decisions, owners, and deadlines.
