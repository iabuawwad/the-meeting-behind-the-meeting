# Body Language - Julius Fast Operating Reference

This reference adapts broad kinesics concepts into a public-safe operating lens for meeting analysis. It does not reproduce source text or treat body language as proof of hidden intent.

## Main Use In The Skill

Use this reference when the transcript includes behavioral, visual, spatial, touch, posture, gesture, orientation, pause, interruption, or tone markers. It supports careful analysis of how nonverbal behavior may align with, complicate, or contradict spoken content.

## Signals To Look For

- Kinesics: posture, gesture, facial expression, movement, orientation, eye behavior, and rhythm.
- Context: meeting type, relationship, seniority, role, culture, setting, and prior baseline.
- Territory: who controls the room, agenda, screen share, seating, speaking space, or decision space.
- Space: distance, leaning in or away, physical withdrawal, crowding, or changes in proximity.
- Touch: handshake, supportive touch, boundary crossing, or absence of expected contact when visual notes include it.
- Body and word contradiction: verbal agreement paired with observable withdrawal, rigidity, hesitation, or avoidance markers.
- Cultural caution: behavior that may differ by culture, setting, accessibility, neurodiversity, or remote-work norms.

## Output Sections It Supports

- Nonverbal behavioral signals
- Said vs meant hypotheses
- Participation and influence observations
- Relationship risk
- Transcript quality caveats when behavioral markers are absent or weak

## Guardrails

- Do not analyze body language unless the source includes behavioral, visual, pause, interruption, or tone markers.
- Do not treat any single gesture as proof.
- Do not claim someone is lying, hiding intent, attracted, hostile, submissive, dominant, or psychologically unsafe based only on body language.
- Do not ignore culture, role, environment, disability, remote latency, or transcript quality.
- Use body-word contradiction as a clarification prompt, not an accusation.

## Confidence Rules

- High confidence: repeated behavioral markers form a cluster, the trigger is clear, and the transcript has strong integrity.
- Medium confidence: the behavior is observable but context or baseline is incomplete.
- Low confidence: only one marker is present, the transcript is text-heavy, or alternative explanations are strong.
- No confidence: the transcript has no behavioral, visual, pause, interruption, or tone markers.

## When Not To Use This Reference

- Plain text transcripts with no behavioral markers.
- Requests for lie detection or hidden-intent certainty.
- Situations where cultural context is central but absent.
- Decisions that require legal, medical, HR, or disciplinary judgment without qualified human review.
