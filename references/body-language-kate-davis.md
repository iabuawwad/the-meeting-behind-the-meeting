# Body Language - Kate Davis Operating Reference

This reference adapts practical emotional-intelligence and observation themes into a public-safe operating lens. It excludes overconfident claims about speed reading, mind control, dark psychology, or manipulation.

## Main Use In The Skill

Use this reference to support self-awareness, self-regulation, empathy, observation caution, and manipulation-awareness guardrails during meeting analysis. It is most useful when the transcript shows conflict, defensiveness, unclear emotional tone, or persuasion pressure.

## Signals To Look For

- Self-awareness: speakers acknowledge uncertainty, reaction, limits, or their own role in a problem.
- Self-regulation: speakers pause, de-escalate, clarify, or repair after tension.
- Empathy: speakers reflect another person's concern before responding.
- Active listening: speakers answer the actual question, ask clarifying questions, or summarize fairly.
- Emotional escalation: repeated interruption, sharp wording, dismissive phrasing, or pressure tactics.
- Manipulation-awareness: urgency without evidence, false binary choices, guilt pressure, intimidation, flattery used to bypass scrutiny, or vague appeals to trust.
- Observation caution: places where the transcript does not support a confident emotional or behavioral claim.

## Output Sections It Supports

- Transcript quality caveats
- Said vs meant hypotheses
- Psychological safety signals
- Relationship risk
- Influence and participation map
- Next meeting playbook

## Guardrails

- Use observation to ask better questions, not to claim certainty about another person.
- Do not use "dark psychology," NLP, speed-reading, or manipulation concepts as proof.
- Do not recommend manipulative tactics.
- Do not label a person as emotionally intelligent or unintelligent from one transcript.
- Do not infer inner emotion from one phrase when a practical alternative explanation exists.

## Confidence Rules

- High confidence: a pattern appears across multiple exchanges and is directly supported by words or markers.
- Medium confidence: one clear exchange supports the observation, but context is incomplete.
- Low confidence: the signal is ambiguous, tone is unavailable, or the transcript quality gate limits interpretation.
- Require lower confidence for emotional claims than for factual reconstruction.

## When Not To Use This Reference

- When the user wants a factual decision log only.
- When the request asks for clinical mental-health assessment, manipulation coaching, or certainty about motive.
- When transcript quality is too low to evaluate emotional or conversational patterns.
- When the evidence is limited to a single ambiguous word or pause.
