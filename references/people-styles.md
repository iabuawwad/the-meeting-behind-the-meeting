# People Styles Operating Reference

This reference adapts the behavior-based People Styles model into a meeting-analysis lens. It describes communication patterns in context, not personality types.

## Main Use In The Skill

Use this reference to map how participants appear to communicate in a specific transcript and how to adapt the next meeting accordingly. It supports style-flex, not labeling.

## Signals To Look For

- Assertiveness: directness, speed of decision, willingness to challenge, control of agenda, and force of requests.
- Responsiveness: warmth, emotional expressiveness, relationship language, listening behavior, and attention to impact on people.
- Analytical pattern: lower assertiveness and lower responsiveness; evidence, precision, process, risk, and careful evaluation.
- Driver pattern: higher assertiveness and lower responsiveness; outcomes, speed, control, decisions, and accountability.
- Amiable pattern: lower assertiveness and higher responsiveness; harmony, inclusion, support, and relationship stability.
- Expressive pattern: higher assertiveness and higher responsiveness; vision, energy, storytelling, possibilities, and momentum.
- Style-flex opportunities: places where communication should be adjusted for clarity, relationship, speed, or evidence.

## Output Sections It Supports

- Communication style map
- Participation and influence map
- Stakeholder alignment
- Relationship risk
- Next meeting playbook
- Recommended opening, questions, and closing

## Guardrails

- Do not label personality.
- Do not say a participant "is" a style. Say the transcript shows a style pattern in this context.
- Do not rank styles as better or worse.
- Do not infer values, intelligence, competence, or intent from style.
- Use style-flex ethically: adapt for understanding, not manipulation.

## Confidence Rules

- High confidence: repeated communication behavior appears across several exchanges.
- Medium confidence: the style pattern appears clearly in one meeting segment.
- Low confidence: the speaker has few turns, the transcript is short, or role expectations may explain the behavior.
- Lower confidence when hierarchy, time pressure, legal constraints, or technical complexity may be driving the style.

## When Not To Use This Reference

- When there are too few speaker turns to identify behavior patterns.
- When the task is only to summarize decisions or action items.
- When the user wants personality profiling.
- When transcript quality makes speaker attribution unreliable.
