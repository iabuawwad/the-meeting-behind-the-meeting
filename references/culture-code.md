# Culture Code Operating Reference

This reference adapts group-culture concepts into a meeting-level lens. It assesses interaction patterns in the transcript, not the entire organization.

## Main Use In The Skill

Use this reference when analyzing safety, belonging, vulnerability loops, shared purpose, group interaction, and participation balance in a meeting or recurring meeting pattern.

## Signals To Look For

- Safety: participants can raise concerns, ask questions, and name risk without immediate blame.
- Belonging cues: acknowledgment, invitation, inclusion, listening, and response to contributions.
- Vulnerability loops: someone admits uncertainty, risk, error, or need for help and receives a constructive response.
- Shared purpose: participants refer to a common goal, customer outcome, mission, standard, or decision criterion.
- Group interaction: short useful exchanges, building on ideas, direct repair, and clear handoffs.
- Participation balance: who speaks, who asks, who decides, who is silent, and who is affected but absent.
- Friction signals: ignored questions, status anxiety, blame, defensive routines, or repeated avoidance of bad news.

## Output Sections It Supports

- Culture and psychological safety signals
- Participation balance
- Relationship risk
- Decision risk
- Next meeting playbook
- Recommended questions

## Guardrails

- Do not generalize from one meeting to the whole organization.
- Do not diagnose culture.
- Do not treat silence as unsafe by default; consider role, agenda, expertise, seniority, time pressure, and transcript gaps.
- Do not label people as safe or unsafe.
- Use the analysis to improve meeting conditions and decision quality.

## Confidence Rules

- High confidence: repeated interaction patterns appear across the meeting or multiple supplied meetings.
- Medium confidence: clear signals appear in one meeting but broader context is missing.
- Low confidence: participation data is incomplete or speaker labels are unreliable.
- Lower confidence for culture claims than for meeting-level interaction observations.

## When Not To Use This Reference

- When there is no group interaction, such as a one-way voice note.
- When the transcript is too short to assess participation.
- When the user asks for broad organizational diagnosis.
- When the only available evidence is a summary written after the meeting.
