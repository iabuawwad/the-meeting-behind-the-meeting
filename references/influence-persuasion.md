# Influence Persuasion Operating Reference

This reference adapts influence and persuasion concepts into an ethical meeting-analysis lens. It focuses on how participants move decisions and participation, not on manipulation.

## Main Use In The Skill

Use this reference when analyzing who shaped the meeting, how agreement was built, why a decision moved or stalled, and what persuasion patterns should be handled ethically in the next meeting.

## Signals To Look For

- Positional power: title, authority, ownership of resources, approval rights, or agenda control.
- Emotion: stated urgency, concern, enthusiasm, frustration, or reassurance that changes the meeting direction.
- Expertise: specialized knowledge, technical explanation, data, or others deferring to subject-matter judgment.
- Conversational control: opening topics, closing topics, reframing, summarizing, interrupting, redirecting, or deciding sequence.
- Liking: rapport, similarity, compliments, familiarity, or goodwill that may increase receptivity.
- Reciprocity: favors, concessions, support, or "we helped you, now..." dynamics.
- Social proof: references to other teams, customers, competitors, leaders, or market behavior.
- Consistency: appeals to prior commitments, values, roadmap, policy, or previous decisions.
- Authority: references to leaders, experts, legal, compliance, standards, or external validation.
- Scarcity: limited time, budget, capacity, access, or opportunity.
- Ethical persuasion: transparency, respect for autonomy, accurate evidence, and fair framing.

## Output Sections It Supports

- Influence and participation map
- Decision accountability
- Risk register
- Said vs meant hypotheses
- Next meeting playbook
- Recommended questions and likely objections

## Guardrails

- Do not recommend manipulation, pressure tactics, or concealed influence.
- Do not treat persuasion as proof that a decision is good or bad.
- Do not infer emotional state unless words, tone markers, or behavior support it.
- Separate formal authority from actual conversational influence.
- Identify who did not participate, not only who spoke most.

## Confidence Rules

- High confidence: influence behavior is visible across multiple turns and affects the outcome.
- Medium confidence: one clear influence move appears, but its effect is uncertain.
- Low confidence: a persuasion pattern may be present but could be explained by role, agenda, or missing context.
- Reduce confidence when the transcript lacks speaker labels or complete turn-taking.

## When Not To Use This Reference

- When the task is only factual reconstruction.
- When the user asks for coercive persuasion or manipulation tactics.
- When participation cannot be measured because the transcript is incomplete.
- When influence claims would rely on private motive rather than observable behavior.
