# What Every Body Is Saying Operating Reference

This reference adapts systematic nonverbal observation concepts into a public-safe meeting-analysis lens. It emphasizes baseline, change, clusters, and context instead of single-signal conclusions.

## Main Use In The Skill

Use this reference when the source includes behavioral, visual, pause, interruption, or tone markers and the analysis needs comfort/discomfort or stress-response hypotheses.

## Signals To Look For

- Baseline: the participant's normal behavior within the meeting.
- Trigger: the topic, question, person, decision, or moment that preceded a behavioral change.
- Cluster: multiple related markers appearing together.
- Context: role, culture, setting, remote latency, language, accessibility, topic sensitivity, and power dynamics.
- Comfort: relaxed participation, open engagement, steady response, constructive turn-taking, or reduced defensiveness.
- Discomfort: withdrawal, blocking, long silence, repeated self-correction, abrupt topic shift, or tension markers.
- Freeze: reduced movement, silence, minimal reply, or stalled answer after a trigger.
- Flight: distancing, topic avoidance, deferral, exit, or rapid movement away from the issue.
- Fight: sharp challenge, interruption, escalation, or confrontational exchange.
- Pacify: self-soothing, softening, laughter after tension, repeated reassurance, or smoothing language.

## Output Sections It Supports

- Nonverbal behavioral signals
- Voice, tone, and presence observations
- Said vs meant hypotheses
- Risk register
- Next meeting playbook

## Guardrails

- Do not draw conclusions from a single signal.
- Do not claim to verify truthfulness from body language.
- Do not claim to know motive or hidden intent.
- Do not use body language without behavioral, visual, pause, interruption, or tone markers.
- Do not ignore baseline, trigger, cluster, and context.
- Treat discomfort as a meeting-state hypothesis, not proof of guilt, deception, or bad faith.

## Confidence Rules

- High confidence: baseline is known, trigger is clear, multiple markers cluster, and alternatives are weaker.
- Medium confidence: a cluster is present but baseline or context is incomplete.
- Low confidence: one or two ambiguous markers exist, or transcript quality is limited.
- No confidence: plain text transcript with no relevant markers.

## When Not To Use This Reference

- Plain text with no behavioral or delivery markers.
- Requests for truthfulness certainty or clinical mental-health assessment.
- High-stakes decisions where nonverbal analysis would be the main evidence.
- Cases where cultural, disability, language, or environmental context is missing and material.
