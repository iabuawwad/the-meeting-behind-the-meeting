# 09 - Influence Participation

Use this framework to analyze how influence and participation appear in a meeting. Focus on observable conversation patterns.

## Influence Sources

### Positional Power

Signals:

- Formal title or decision authority.
- Others defer to the participant.
- The participant assigns work, approves direction, or closes topics.

### Emotion

Signals:

- Frustration, enthusiasm, concern, urgency, or reassurance stated directly or shown through transcript markers.
- Emotional language changes the direction or urgency of the discussion.

Guardrail:

- Do not infer emotion unless words, tone markers, or behavior support it.

### Expertise

Signals:

- Specialized knowledge.
- Others ask for evaluation or validation.
- The participant explains constraints, tradeoffs, or facts.

### Conversational Control

Signals:

- Opens or closes topics.
- Interrupts, redirects, summarizes, or frames choices.
- Controls time, agenda, or decision sequence.

## Persuasion Patterns

### Reciprocity

Signals:

- Favors, concessions, help, or prior support are referenced.

### Social Proof

Signals:

- References to what other teams, customers, leaders, or peers are doing.

### Consistency

Signals:

- Appeals to prior commitments, stated values, roadmap, or previous decisions.

### Authority

Signals:

- References to leadership, policy, expert review, legal, compliance, or external standards.

### Scarcity

Signals:

- Limited time, budget, access, capacity, market window, or opportunity.

## Participation Balance

Assess:

- Who speaks most.
- Who asks questions.
- Who answers or decides.
- Who is silent on topics that affect them.
- Whether disagreement is explored or bypassed.
- Whether action ownership is concentrated or distributed.

## Output Requirements

For each influence observation:

- Evidence
- Influence source or persuasion pattern
- Effect on the meeting
- Alternative explanation
- Confidence
- Recommended action
