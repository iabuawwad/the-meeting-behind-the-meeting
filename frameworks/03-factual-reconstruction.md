# 03 - Factual Reconstruction

Use factual reconstruction before interpretation. The goal is to create a reliable account of what happened in the conversation.

## Who Said What

Capture:

- Speaker name or label.
- Role or affiliation if stated.
- Main claims, asks, objections, answers, and commitments.
- Direct quotes for important points when available.

Rules:

- Preserve uncertainty for unclear speaker labels.
- Do not merge speakers unless the transcript supports it.
- Do not infer unstated roles unless clearly implied by context and labeled as inference.

## Topic Sequence

Rebuild the conversation in order.

For each topic:

- Topic title
- Start and end point if available
- Speakers involved
- Main facts discussed
- Outcome: decided, deferred, unresolved, or unclear

## Questions and Answers

Map important question-and-answer pairs.

For each question:

- Question asked
- Speaker who asked
- Person or group addressed
- Answer given
- Whether the answer was direct, partial, avoided, deferred, or unanswered
- Follow-up required

## Confirmed Decisions

Record only decisions supported by transcript evidence.

For each decision:

- Decision statement
- Decision maker or approving group
- Evidence
- Date or timing if stated
- Implementation owner if assigned
- Dependencies or conditions

If a decision is implied but not confirmed, classify it as a likely decision candidate, not a confirmed decision.

## Open Items

Record unresolved work.

For each item:

- Open question or task
- Owner if stated
- Deadline if stated
- Needed output
- Risk if left unresolved

## Contradictions

Identify contradictions without overstating intent.

Types:

- Factual contradiction
- Timeline contradiction
- Ownership contradiction
- Scope contradiction
- Priority contradiction
- Commitment contradiction

For each contradiction:

- Quote or fact A
- Quote or fact B
- Why they conflict
- Possible reconciliation
- Recommended clarification
