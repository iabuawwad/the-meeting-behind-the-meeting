# 06 - Said vs Meant

Use this framework for evidence-based implied-meaning analysis. It is for practical interpretation, not certainty about private intent.

## Purpose

Identify where a speaker's literal words may carry an operational implication, unstated concern, constraint, or request that should be clarified.

## Required Inputs

- Exact statement or close paraphrase.
- Speaker and context.
- Preceding question or topic.
- Relevant follow-up response.
- Transcript quality and tone confidence.

## Analysis Structure

For each item:

- Said: What was stated.
- Context: What was happening when it was stated.
- Possible meaning: What it may imply.
- Evidence: Transcript facts supporting the possible meaning.
- Alternative explanation: A reasonable different reading.
- Confidence: high, medium, or low.
- Recommended response: A clarification or action.

## Common Evidence-Based Patterns

### Constraint Signal

The speaker mentions timing, capacity, budget, approval, or dependency.

Possible meaning:

- There may be a blocker or boundary that needs explicit handling.

### Concern Signal

The speaker repeats a question, requests evidence, or asks for assurance.

Possible meaning:

- The speaker may not yet be comfortable with the plan.

### Deferral Signal

The speaker says a topic should be handled later or by someone else.

Possible meaning:

- Authority, readiness, or ownership may be unclear.

### Ambiguity Signal

The speaker uses vague agreement, broad endorsement, or passive wording.

Possible meaning:

- The commitment may not yet be operationally defined.

## Guardrails

- Do not claim to know what someone secretly meant.
- Do not use this framework for private-thought claims, truthfulness certainty, or character judgment.
- Do not infer motive from a single phrase.
- Prefer "may mean" over "means" unless the speaker confirms it.
- Always include an alternative explanation for sensitive or high-impact interpretations.
- Convert implied meaning into a clarifying question or practical next step.
