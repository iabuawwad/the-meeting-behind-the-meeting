# 11 - Voice Tone Presence

Use this framework only when the transcript contains tone, pause, interruption, emphasis, audio, or delivery markers. Text alone rarely supports strong tone conclusions.

## Voice and Presence Factors

### Clarity

Signals:

- Direct wording.
- Clear asks.
- Low ambiguity.
- Summaries that improve shared understanding.

### Pace

Signals:

- Fast or slow speech markers.
- Rushed transitions.
- Pauses before key answers.
- Time pressure comments.

### Volume

Signals:

- Transcript notes such as quiet, loud, raised voice, or hard to hear.
- Repeated requests to repeat.

### Authority

Signals:

- Decisive framing.
- Clear ownership.
- Calm command of topic.
- Others accept summaries or decisions.

Guardrail:

- Authority is a communication effect in this meeting, not a personality trait.

### Hesitation

Signals:

- False starts.
- Long pauses.
- Repeated qualifiers.
- Self-corrections.

Alternative explanations:

- Careful wording, uncertainty, poor audio, politeness, or complex subject matter.

### Over-Explaining

Signals:

- Extended justification after a point was accepted.
- Repeated restatement.
- Excessive detail relative to the question.

Alternative explanations:

- Technical complexity, legal sensitivity, new audience, or transcript fragmentation.

### Weak Endings

Signals:

- Statements trail into uncertainty.
- Decisions end without owner or next step.
- Speaker softens a clear recommendation at the end.

### Confidence

Signals:

- Specific claims.
- Stable position.
- Clear evidence.
- Direct answer to challenge.

Guardrail:

- Do not infer inner confidence from style alone.

### Warmth

Signals:

- Acknowledgment.
- Appreciation.
- Inclusive language.
- Repair after disagreement.

## Output Requirements

For each tone or presence claim:

- Name the marker.
- Cite the transcript evidence.
- Rate tone confidence.
- Include an alternative explanation.
- Convert the observation into a practical communication recommendation.
