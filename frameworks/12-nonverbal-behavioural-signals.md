# 12 - Nonverbal Behavioural Signals

Use this framework only when the source includes behavioural, visual, pause, interruption, or tone markers. Do not perform nonverbal analysis from plain text alone.

## Minimum Evidence Requirement

Allowed inputs:

- Video notes.
- Observer notes.
- Transcript markers such as pause, long silence, interruption, overlap, laughter, sigh, raised voice, or looking away.
- Meeting platform annotations.
- User-provided behavioural observations.

Not enough:

- Plain transcript text with no behavioural markers.
- A participant disagreeing or asking questions.
- A single ambiguous pause with no context.

## Analysis Elements

### Baseline

Establish the participant's normal behavior in this meeting before interpreting a change.

Question:

- What was typical for this participant earlier in the same context?

### Trigger

Identify what happened immediately before the behavior.

Question:

- Which question, topic, person, or decision preceded the marker?

### Cluster

Look for multiple related markers, not isolated behavior.

Examples:

- Silence plus topic change plus avoided answer.
- Interruption plus raised volume plus repeated objection.
- Long pause plus qualified answer plus later contradiction.

### Context

Interpret behavior through meeting context.

Consider:

- Role
- Culture
- language fluency
- technical complexity
- remote meeting delay
- audio quality
- stress or time pressure stated in the transcript

### Comfort or Discomfort

Use only as a tentative meeting-state signal.

Allowed:

- "The cluster may show discomfort with the proposal."

Not allowed:

- "The speaker was hiding the truth."

### Freeze, Flight, Fight, Pacify

Use these as descriptive behavioral categories only when evidence supports them.

- Freeze: silence, stalled response, minimal reply after a trigger.
- Flight: topic avoidance, exit, deferral, rapid shift away from issue.
- Fight: challenge, interruption, sharp disagreement, defensive exchange.
- Pacify: softening, reassurance, laughter after tension, repeated agreement.

Guardrail:

- These are not diagnoses and do not prove motive.
