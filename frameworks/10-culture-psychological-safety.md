# 10 - Culture Psychological Safety

Use this framework to assess meeting-level safety and culture signals. Do not make broad claims about an organization from one transcript.

## Belonging Cues

Signals:

- Participants are invited into the discussion.
- Names, roles, and contributions are acknowledged.
- Questions are treated as legitimate.
- People respond to each other rather than talking past each other.

Risks:

- Repeated exclusion.
- Ignored questions.
- Only senior voices shape outcomes.

## Vulnerability Loops

Signals:

- Someone admits uncertainty, a mistake, concern, or need for help.
- Others respond constructively.
- The exchange leads to clarity, support, or better decisions.

Risks:

- Vulnerability is ignored, punished, mocked, or quickly buried.

## Psychological Safety

Meeting-level indicators:

- People can raise concerns.
- Bad news can be stated without immediate blame.
- Dissent receives a response.
- Clarifying questions are welcome.
- Risks are named before decisions are finalized.

Guardrail:

- Assess only the meeting evidence. Do not diagnose the whole culture.

## Shared Purpose

Signals:

- Participants refer to common goals.
- Tradeoffs are evaluated against shared criteria.
- The group returns to the objective after disagreement.

Risks:

- Different participants optimize for different goals without naming the conflict.

## Participation Balance

Assess:

- Speaking distribution.
- Decision participation.
- Question participation.
- Whether affected participants are heard.
- Whether silence might reflect role, agenda, hierarchy, transcript gaps, or lack of safety.
